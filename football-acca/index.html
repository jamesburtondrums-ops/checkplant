Methodology and limitations

1. Season data. Complete results and upcoming fixtures are taken from open-matchday for the 2026/27 season. The dataset currently covers 75 competitions and is refreshed daily. The data is licensed for informational, non-commercial use with attribution. Missing fields stay missing.

2. Expected goals. For each fixture we estimate home and away scoring rates from current-season home/away attacking and defensive results, pulling small samples back towards each league's observed goal averages. The latest five matches modestly affect estimates, and rates are capped to reduce extreme forecasts. The model then uses a Poisson scoreline grid (0–9 goals) to calculate BTTS, each match winner, and their intersections. We do not call this real xG.

3. Why the combined market is different. BTTS + Home Win is the sum of scoreline probabilities where both sides score and the home side wins. It is not BTTS probability multiplied by home-win probability: those outcomes are related within a match.

4. Fair odds and value. Fair odds = 1 / model probability. Expected value per £1 at decimal odds d is (probability × d) − 1. Positive estimated EV is not proof of bookmaker error because predictions may be miscalibrated. No real bookmaker odds are retrieved; you enter them manually.

5. Form and players. Results, goals, home/away splits and some shot statistics are available. The dataset does not contain reliably complete injury reports, starting XIs, player minutes, assists or individual xG. Named goal scorers are shown only where recorded; incomplete coverage is labelled.

6. Evidence index. The 0–100 evidence index combines sample size, venue record, recent form, league position, historical H2H and dated source-linked team news. Unmodelled reported absences reduce it, and scores are capped because starting XIs have not been confirmed. It has not been validated as a probabilistic confidence measure. Missing team news caps confidence; archived H2H is for context only, and injury reports are not assumed to confirm final squads.

7. Risks. Small samples, managerial changes, red cards, travel, fixture congestion and lineup changes may invalidate model assumptions. Cup and cross-league fixtures are not inherently comparable. Backtests use historical estimates but no guaranteed future edge. Accas compound uncertainty and can have higher variance than singles.

8. Independence and freshness. Two picks from different matches are treated as independent for combined probabilities, which is an approximation. File dates are from the publishing source and are not live scores. The source's kickoff time convention can be inaccurate for UK local times; date-only fixtures are shown here.

Future improvements. Integrate a licensed player/lineup/injury data provider, compare opening and current odds, implement proper out-of-sample calibration and bookmaker closing-line testing, then track settled bets.
